# Good vs Bad Figures

## A Good Figure

A good figure makes one claim clearly.

It has:
- One primary question or thesis
- A visual form that directly proves the thesis
- An obvious focal point
- Minimal decoding before the takeaway lands
- Labels placed where the eye already goes
- Numbers used as punchlines, not clutter
- Text that supports the figure instead of explaining the whole figure
- Enough contrast that the important difference is visible immediately

The best figures feel inevitable: the title says the claim, the visual shows the evidence, and the annotation names the result.

## A Bad Figure

A bad figure shows data without creating insight.

It often has:
- Too many competing messages
- A chart that only restates the subtitle
- Process bookkeeping instead of a real result
- Weak visual contrast or nearly identical bars/cells
- Categories that require too much decoding
- Legends, notes, or captions doing the main work
- Counts shown without normalization or context
- Large empty plotting areas with little informational payoff
- A focal point that is unclear or absent

Bad figures may be technically correct, but they do not earn their space. They are often better as a sentence, table, inset, or appendix item.
